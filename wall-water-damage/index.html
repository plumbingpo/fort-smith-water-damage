Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Fort Smith

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Plenty of walls that get wet can stay. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

What It Looks Like

  • Trim along the floor that has puffed up, twisted or come loose
  • Caulk lines cracking or gaps opening where trim meets the wall
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Cut or Ventilate

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. Walls that cannot be kept get a flood cut along a level line, with the wet insulation pulled.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on, usually 3 to 5 days, until the studs and the drywall left in place test dry. After that comes insulation, new drywall hung and finished, trim put back and paint.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Hidden Behind Cabinets

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Meter readings at the toe kick and from the far side of the wall reveal it.

Spread Into Adjoining Rooms

Water passes under the bottom plate into the next room or closet. Our team inspects each wet wall from both sides, plus the floor running beside it.

Mold Inside Cavities

Paper lining, darkness and stagnant air make the hidden side of a wall a perfect place for mold. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. Baseboards and the bottom plate take on moisture.

24 to 48 hours

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Inside the cavity, the paper face becomes a place mold can begin.

Days 3 through 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.
